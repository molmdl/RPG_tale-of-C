**Result:** All 4 ETC complexes have a VERIFIED disease-causing point mutation whose residue is present (with the correct wild-type identity) in the available wild-type PDB structure, so `cmd.alter` (via `c14/pymol_layer/edit_ops.py:apply_edit`) can target it at runtime. All 4 should be/become edit-allowed.

**Important note on "Mutant PDB?":** A search of RCSB confirms there are **NO solved PDB structures of any disease-mutant form of the human (or mammalian) ETC complexes** — every available ETC PDB is either wild-type or an engineered catalytic-site mutant for mechanistic enzymology (e.g. E. coli SDH active-site mutants), none representing a human disease allele. The disease state must therefore be applied at runtime via `cmd.alter` on the loaded wild-type PDB (the Phase-4 `apply_edit` helper). **Geometry caveat (per REPLAN-CONTEXT §1):** `cmd.alter` changes residue identity but does NOT repack side chains or relax the backbone, so the "mutated" structure has the correct sequence but APPROXIMATE 3D coordinates. `text_teaching` should note this is a simplified representation.

## Per-Complex Detailed Findings

### Complex I — `etc.complex_i` (already edit-allowed; needs real signature)

**PDB:** 5LDW — "Structure of mammalian respiratory Complex I, class1" (Vinothkumar et al. 2016, *Nature*). Resolution 4.27 Å. **Organism: Bos taurus (bovine)** (verified via RCSB `entity_src_nat.pdbx_ncbi_taxonomy_id=9913`). 44 polymer entities, single monomer (assembly_count=1). All 7 mtDNA subunits (MT-ND1/2/3/4/4L/5/6) + all core nuclear iron-sulfur/flavoprotein subunits (NDUFV1, NDUFV2, NDUFS1/2/3/7/8) + accessory subunits present.

**Subunit chosen:** NDUFS8 — the "TYKY" iron-sulfur subunit, contains two [4Fe-4S] clusters (the N2 cluster is the terminal electron donor to ubiquinone). Bovine UniProt P42028; human UniProt **O00217**. Present in 5LDW as chain **I**.

**1. Known disease-causing POINT MUTATION:** YES.
- **NDUFS8 R102H** — human UniProt O00217 position 102 (full precursor). Variant ID VAR_019539.
- dbSNP rs121912638.
- **Disease:** Mitochondrial complex I deficiency, nuclear type 2 (MC1DN2), MIM 618222. Description (UniProt): "A form of mitochondrial complex I deficiency, the most common biochemical signature of mitochondrial disorders… Clinical disorders have variable severity, ranging from lethal neonatal disease to adult-onset neurodegenerative disorders. Phenotypes include macrocephaly with progressive leukodystrophy, non-specific encephalopathy, cardiomyopathy, myopathy, liver disease, **Leigh syndrome**, Leber hereditary optic neuropathy, and some forms of Parkinson disease."

**2. Mutant PDB?** NO. No disease-mutant Complex I structure exists in the RCSB. The mutation must be applied at runtime via `cmd.alter`.

**3. Residue present in wild-type PDB?** YES — VERIFIED.
- Human UniProt O00217 (length 210) vs 5LDW chain I (PDB resi 1–176, 176 modeled residues): best 1:1 ungapped alignment, offset = −34 (i.e. human presequence = 34 aa; bovine mature protein starts at PDB resi 1). Identity = 169/176 = **96.0%** of overlapping region.
- Human pos 102 (R, ARG) → PDB chain I resi **68**. Verified: 5LDW chain I residue 68 = **ARG** ✓.
- This means the disease residue is in the well-ordered core of NDUFS8 (not a missing loop, not an absent subunit). `cmd.alter("resi 68 and chain I", "resn='HIS'")` will target it.

**4. REVERSE-MUTATION (the "correct fix" the player performs):** `H68R` in PDB notation.
- Disease state (on_enter pre-edit): 5LDW chain I resi 68 = **HIS** (after `apply_edit` mutates ARG→HIS).
- Player's correct fix: change HIS → ARG (the wild-type) at chain I resi 68.
- **edits.json signature:**
  ```json
  {"op":"point_mutation","target":"resi 68 and chain I","args":{"new_res":"ARG"}}
  ```
- Human-notation label (for narrative text): R102H → H102R (or simply "the R102H Leigh mutation; the player restores R102").

**5. Disease (narrative stakes):** MC1DN2 / Leigh-spectrum mitochondrial encephalopathy. **Historically significant** — PMID 9837812 (Loeffen 1998, *Am J Hum Genet*): "First nuclear encoded complex I mutation in a patient with Leigh syndrome." This was the FIRST EVER nuclear-encoded Complex I mutation found in a Leigh patient — a landmark in mitochondrial medicine. NDUFS8 is the terminal iron-sulfur protein in the electron-transfer chain to ubiquinone; R102H "decreases enzyme activity" and "impairs assembly of complex I" (UniProt).

**6. Source / claim_id CANDIDATE:**
- UniProt: https://rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/O00217 (variant VAR_019539, evidence → PubMed 9837812)
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9837812/ — Loeffen J et al. 1998, *Am J Hum Genet* 63(6):1598–1608
- ClinVar: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/clinvar/?term=7512 — ClinVar Variation ID 7512. **Classification: "Conflicting classifications of pathogenicity"** (last evaluated 2024-05-23; trait "not provided"). Note: ClinVar conflict reflects the variant's rarity and incomplete penetrance data; UniProt + the original paper are unambiguous about disease causation.
- dbSNP: rs121912638 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/snp/rs121912638
- **CANDIDATE claim_id: `DIS-NDUFS8-01-cand`** — pending human per-claim approval.

**Alternatives considered (also verified present in 5LDW):**
- **NDUFS7 V122M** (MC1DN3, PMID 10360771 Triepels 1999, dbSNP rs104894705) — PDB 5LDW chain B resi 88 = VAL ✓. ClinVar 7681: "Conflicting classifications". Subunit PSST — also an iron-sulfur protein.
- **NDUFS7 R145H** (Leigh syndrome, PMID 17275378 Lebon 2007, dbSNP rs121434479) — PDB 5LDW chain B resi 111 = ARG ✓. UniProt notes "uncertain significance". Same subunit as V122M.
- **NDUFS3 R140W** (MC1DN8, PMID 30140060 Lou 2018, dbSNP rs142248674) — PDB 5LDW chain C resi 104 = ARG ✓. UniProt notes "uncertain significance".
- **NDUFV1 A341V / E214K / T423M** (MC1DN4) — all three residues verified present in 5LDW chain F (resi 321/194/403 respectively, all matching wild-type). NDUFV1 is the 51kDa FMN/NADH-binding flavoprotein. **Strong alternative** if the human prefers a catalytic-flavoprotein narrative.

**Why NDUFS8 R102H was chosen as primary:** the Loeffen 1998 paper is historically the FIRST nuclear Complex I Leigh mutation (highest clinical-literature prominence), and NDUFS8's N2 [4Fe-4S] cluster is the terminal electron donor to ubiquinone — narratively the "last hand-off" in Complex I before the hero's electrons leave for Complex III. If the human prefers NDUFV1 (the NADH-binding entry subunit — "first hand-off") that is equally verified and can be swapped.

### Complex II — `etc.complex_ii` (NEW candidate for edit-allowed)

**PDB:** 1ZOY — "Crystal Structure of Mitochondrial Respiratory Complex II from porcine heart at 2.4 Angstroms" (Sun et al. 2005, *Cell*). Resolution 2.4 Å. **Organism: Sus scrofa (pig)**. 4 polymer entities — the canonical SDH tetramer: chain A = SDHA (FAD-binding flavoprotein, UniProt Q0QF01), chain B = SDHB (iron-sulfur protein, UniProt Q007T0), chain C = SDHC (large cytochrome b, UniProt D0VWV4), chain D = SDHD (small cytochrome b, UniProt A5GZW8). Single monomer.

**Subunit chosen:** SDHA — the catalytic flavoprotein (binds FAD, oxidizes succinate to fumarate). Human UniProt **P31040**. Present in 1ZOY as chain **A**.

**1. Known disease-causing POINT MUTATION:** YES.
- **SDHA R554W** — human UniProt P31040 position 554 (full precursor). Variant ID VAR_002449.
- dbSNP rs9809219.
- **Disease:** **Leigh syndrome (LS)**, MIM 256000. UniProt: "An early-onset progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by the presence of focal, bilateral lesions in one or more areas of the central nervous system including the brainstem, thalamus, basal ganglia, cerebellum and spinal cord." The same allele is also associated with MC2DN1 (Complex II deficiency nuclear type 1), PPGL5 (hereditary paraganglioma syndrome 5), NDAXOA (neurodegeneration with ataxia and late-onset optic atrophy), and CMD1GG (dilated cardiomyopathy 1GG) — a pleiotropic allele, but the LS association is the cleanest match for the restoration narrative.

**2. Mutant PDB?** NO. No disease-mutant SDH structure exists in the RCSB.

**3. Residue present in wild-type PDB?** YES — VERIFIED.
- Human UniProt P31040 (length 664) vs pig UniProt Q0QF01 (length 664): 1:1 ungapped alignment, identity = 628/664 = **94.6%**. Residue 554 is conserved (R in both).
- Pig UniProt Q0QF01 → PDB 1ZOY chain A: SIFTS mapping gives pig UniProt 43 = PDB resi 1, so PDB residue = pig UniProt − 42.
- Therefore human UniProt 554 (R) → pig UniProt 554 (R) → PDB 1ZOY chain A resi **512**. Verified: 1ZOY chain A residue 512 = **ARG** ✓.
- 1ZOY chain A spans PDB resi 10–622 (residues 1–9 are the cleaved transit peptide — absent from the structure, but the disease residue at 512 is far inside the modeled region).

**4. REVERSE-MUTATION (the "correct fix" the player performs):** `W512R` in PDB notation.
- Disease state (on_enter pre-edit): 1ZOY chain A resi 512 = **TRP** (after `apply_edit` mutates ARG→TRP).
- Player's correct fix: change TRP → ARG at chain A resi 512.
- **edits.json signature:**
  ```json
  {"op":"point_mutation","target":"resi 512 and chain A","args":{"new_res":"ARG"}}
  ```
- Human-notation label (for narrative text): R554W → W554R.

**5. Disease (narrative stakes):** Leigh syndrome. **Historically significant** — PMID 7550341 (Bourgeron 1995, *Nat Genet*): "Mutation of a nuclear succinate dehydrogenase gene results in mitochondrial respiratory chain deficiency" — the FIRST EVER mutation in a nuclear-encoded respiratory-chain component found in a Leigh patient. (UniProt's LS-disease comment for SDHA cites 3 papers: 10746566 Parfait 2000, 24781757 Renkema 2015, 7550341 Bourgeron 1995. The Parfait 2000 abstract explicitly states "SDH deficiency represents a minor cause of Leigh syndrome" and "the first mutation in a nuclear-encoded respiratory chain component… was reported 4 years ago in two siblings with LS and SDH deficiency" — confirming the Bourgeron 1995 discovery.)

**6. Source / claim_id CANDIDATE:**
- UniProt: https://rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P31040 (variant VAR_002449, evidence → PubMed 7550341)
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7550341/ — Bourgeron T et al. 1995, *Nat Genet* 11(2):144–149 (mutation discovery); https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10746566/ — Parfait B et al. 2000, *Hum Genet* 106(3):236–243 (compound heterozygous SDHA LS patient)

- dbSNP: rs9809219 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/snp/rs9809219
- **CANDIDATE claim_id: `DIS-SDHA-01-cand`** — pending human per-claim approval.

**Alternatives considered (also verified present in 1ZOY chain A):**
- **SDHA C189G** (LS, dbSNP rs1553999752) — PDB chain A resi 147 = CYS ✓
- **SDHA A524V** (LS, dbSNP rs137852767) — PDB chain A resi 482 = ALA ✓
- **SDHA G555E** (MC2DN1 + CMD1GG, dbSNP rs137852768) — PDB chain A resi 513 = GLY ✓ (immediately adjacent to R554W)

- **SDHA R451C** (NDAXOA, dbSNP rs1553999752) — PDB chain A resi 409 = ARG ✓

**Why R554W was chosen as primary:** it is the most clinically prominent — the LS association is explicit in UniProt ("In LS") and ClinVar (Likely pathogenic + Leigh syndrome trait), and it sits on the historical discovery paper trail (Bourgeron 1995 / Parfait 2000). SDHA is the catalytic FAD/succinate-binding subunit — narratively the "succinate-oxidation heart" of Complex II. The pleiotropy (LS + PPGL5 + cardiomyopathy) is interesting but the LS phenotype is the one to use for narrative consistency with Complexes I & IV.

It is characterized by onset in early childhood of episodic acute lactic acidosis, ketoacidosis, and insulin-responsive hyperglycemia, usually associated with infection. This is a "host in metabolic crisis" phenotype that fits the restoration narrative well. The disease gene paper is PMID 23910460 (Gaignard 2013, *Am J Hum Genet*): "Mutations in CYC1 encoding cytochrome c1 subunit of respiratory chain complex III cause insulin-responsive hyperglycemia."

- **COX6B1 R20H** (MC4DN7, dbSNP rs121909602) — PDB 1OCC chain H resi 19 = ARG ✓. PMID 18499082 (Massa 2008, *Am J Hum Genet*: "Severe infantile encephalomyopathy caused by a mutation in COX6B1"). ClinVar 16875: **Likely pathogenic** (last evaluated 2024-05-31, "criteria provided, single submitter"). Disease: MC4DN7 — encephalomyopathy, neurodegeneration, cognitive decline, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. Also strong alternative.

**Why COX4I1 P152T was chosen as primary:** ClinVar Pathogenic + the disease is explicitly "resembling Leigh syndrome" (the Pillai 2019 title), matching the Leigh-spectrum theme of Complexes I & II. COX4I1 is the ATP/ADP-sensing regulatory subunit — narratively "the energy-state sensor of Complex IV". If the human prefers a catalytic subunit (heme-A binding) or a more-recently-reviewed ClinVar entry, **COX5A R107C (ClinVar Pathogenic, 2024 review)** is the recommended swap; if the human prefers the most "encephalomyopathy" phenotype, **COX6B1 R20H (ClinVar Likely pathogenic, 2024)** is the swap.

**Open question — SURF1 (see §Open Questions):** SURF1 (UniProt Q15526) is the MOST COMMON genetic cause of Leigh syndrome with COX deficiency (mutated in ~25% of Leigh-COX cases; many ClinVar Pathogenic alleles e.g. R339X, c.312_321del, fsX280). **But SURF1 is an assembly factor, NOT a structural subunit of Complex IV — it is NOT present in the 1OCC PDB.** `cmd.alter` cannot target a residue that isn't in the loaded structure. Two options for the human: (a) use a structural subunit mutation as above (COX4I1 P152T / COX5A R107C / COX6B1 R20H — all ClinVar Pathogenic/Likely-pathogenic and present in 1OCC); (b) narratively describe SURF1 deficiency as the disease but use a "metaphorical edit" (not a real `cmd.alter` residue change) — this breaks the existing EditRouter mechanic. **Recommendation: option (a).**

## Open Questions for Human Review

These are decisions that the research cannot make unilaterally — they require human judgment on narrative + scientific framing.

### OQ-1: SURF1 (Complex IV assembly factor) vs structural-subunit mutation
**Question:** The MOST COMMON genetic cause of Leigh syndrome with COX deficiency is SURF1 (an assembly factor, ~25% of Leigh-COX cases, many ClinVar Pathogenic alleles). But SURF1 is NOT a structural subunit of Complex IV — it is NOT present in the 1OCC PDB — so `cmd.alter` cannot target it. Should the game:
- **(a) [RECOMMENDED]** Use a structural-subunit mutation that IS in 1OCC (COX4I1 P152T / COX5A R107C / COX6B1 R20H — all ClinVar Pathogenic/Likely-pathogenic). The player makes a real `cmd.alter` edit on the loaded 1OCC structure.
- **(b)** Use SURF1 as the disease gene for narrative accuracy, but treat the "edit" as a metaphorical/scripted action (not a real `cmd.alter` residue change). This breaks the existing EditRouter mechanic (which matches on a PDB-residue signature).
- **(c)** Use SURF1 AND load a separate SURF1 PDB structure (e.g. 5A5L = human SURF1, or 2L7Y) — but this requires adding a second PDB load to the `on_enter` MolActions and a different edit-target mechanism. More work; out of scope for the Phase 5.1 replan.

**Recommendation: (a)** — keep the existing EditRouter mechanic intact, use a structural subunit. COX4I1 P152T (primary) or COX5A R107C (alternative — most recently reviewed ClinVar Pathogenic, 2024).

### OQ-2: BCS1L (Complex III assembly factor) vs MT-CYB / CYC1 structural subunit
**Question:** Same pattern as OQ-1, for Complex III. BCS1L is the MOST clinically prominent Complex III disease gene (MC3DN1, GRACILE, Björnstad) with many mutations (R45C, P99L, R155P, R183C, V327A, etc.) — ClinVar has several Pathogenic alleles. But BCS1L is an assembly factor, NOT present in 1BGY. Should the game:
- **(a) [RECOMMENDED]** Use a structural-subunit mutation present in 1BGY (CYC1 W96C or CYC1 L215F — both ClinVar Pathogenic; or MT-CYB G290D — ClinVar VUS).
- **(b)** Use BCS1L narratively + metaphorical edit (breaks EditRouter).

**Recommendation: (a)** — CYC1 W96C (primary) or CYC1 L215F (alternative — more central residue).

### OQ-3: NDUFS8 R102H (ClinVar "conflicting") vs a ClinVar-Pathogenic Complex I alternative
**Question:** For Complex I, NDUFS8 R102H is the historically most prominent (Loeffen 1998, the FIRST nuclear-encoded Complex I Leigh mutation) but ClinVar says "Conflicting classifications of pathogenicity". None of the verified-present Complex I subunit options I found have clean ClinVar "Pathogenic" status — they are all either "conflicting" or "uncertain significance" or have unresolved PubMed evidence. This likely reflects the rarity of Complex I subunit point mutations (most MC1DN patients have truncating/frameshift mutations, not missense). Options:
- **(a) [RECOMMENDED]** Use NDUFS8 R102H anyway — the UniProt curation + the original Loeffen 1998 paper are unambiguous, and the ClinVar conflict reflects incomplete penetrance data (the variant is rare), not absence of disease causation.
- **(b)** Use NDUFV1 A341V / E214K / T423M (MC1DN4) — the 51kDa FMN/NADH-binding catalytic subunit; ClinVar status for A341V was not cleanly retrievable (the UniProt XML evidence for this variant did not resolve a PubMed ID in my query — flagged for human verification).
- **(c)** Use NDUFS7 V122M (MC1DN3, PMID 10360771 Triepels 1999, ClinVar conflicting) — PSST iron-sulfur subunit.

**Recommendation: (a)** — NDUFS8 R102H. If the human prefers a ClinVar cleaner option, a separate ClinVar query for the NDUFV1 A341V allele should be done before swapping.

### OQ-4: Human vs PDB residue numbering in narrative text
**Question:** The `edits.json` signature MUST use PDB residue numbering (because `cmd.alter` targets the loaded PDB: e.g. "resi 512 and chain A" for SDHA, NOT "resi 554"). But the disease literature and UniProt all use HUMAN precursor numbering (R554W, R102H, W96C, P152T). The narrative text (`text_dramatic` / `text_teaching`) will need to bridge these — e.g. "the disease residue R554 (PDB residue 512 in the structure you see) is mutated to W in Leigh-syndrome patients".
- **Decision for Phase 7 content:** which convention to use in the player-facing text? Recommend: human UniProt numbering (R554W) in narrative text + a clear sidebar/teaching note giving the PDB residue number (512) for the player's actual edit. This is a Phase-7 content decision, not a Phase 5.1 structure decision — flagging here so the planner knows it's pending.

### OQ-5: Two monomers in 1BGY and 1OCC
**Question:** 1BGY and 1OCC each have TWO monomers in the asymmetric unit (1BGY chains A–K + L–V; 1OCC chains A–M + N–Z). The edits.json signature targets a specific chain (e.g. "chain D" for CYC1 in 1BGY, "chain D" for COX4I1 in 1OCC) — this targets only ONE monomer. Options:
- **(a) [RECOMMENDED]** Target one chain only — the player edits one monomer, narratively "fixing the broken half of the dimer". Simple, matches the existing EditRouter signature-matching (exact selector string).
- **(b)** Use a selector that targets both equivalent chains (e.g. "resi 12 and (chain D or chain P)" for CYC1 in 1BGY). More biochemically complete but changes the signature format (the existing edits.json schema uses a single selector string).

**Recommendation: (a)** — target one chain. The Phase-4 EditRouter matches on exact signature dict equality, so a single-chain selector is simplest. Phase-7 content can explain "the disease affects one copy of the dimer; restoring it restores function" if needed.

### OQ-6: Pig vs bovine vs human PDBs
**Question:** The 4 PDB candidates are not all the same organism: 5LDW = bovine, 1ZOY = pig, 1BGY = bovine, 1OCC = bovine. The disease mutations are all from HUMAN genetics (UniProt human entries). The residue mappings (human → pig/bovine → PDB) are verified for each chosen mutation, but the species mix is worth noting for the `README` "cast" list (spec.md requirement 5: "drama-like cast of protein name, PDB ID, and resolution"). The species mismatch is standard practice in structural biology (mammalian orthologs are ~95% identical in the catalytic subunits) and the disease residues are all conserved — but the human should be aware that the "cast" is bovine/pig, not human.
- **Note:** there ARE human ETC PDBs (e.g. 5LDW is the closest to mammalian — actually bovine; 5XTD / 6ZD9 human Complex I; 1SVM avian Complex II; etc.) but they are either lower resolution, partial, or have their own disease-residue gaps. The 4 chosen PDBs are the existing skeleton candidates and are verified to work for the disease mutations. Switching PDBs is out of scope for the Phase 5.1 replan.

### OQ-7: Geometry caveat communication
**Question:** `cmd.alter` changes residue identity but does NOT repack side chains or relax the backbone. The disease-mutant structure shown on_enter will have the correct SEQUENCE but APPROXIMATE 3D coordinates (the mutant side chain is placed in the wild-type backbone conformation). The `text_teaching` should explain this is a simplified representation (per REPLAN-CONTEXT §1, which already mandates this caveat). This is a Phase-7 content decision — flagging here so the planner knows the caveat is required.
